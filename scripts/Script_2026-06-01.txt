Deep dive one. The McKinsey banking framework and what it inherits from history.

The instinct to dismiss this report is to call it another McKinsey AI overlay. Push back. The real story is that McKinsey is acknowledging the end of what its own archive used to call smart followership. In the nineteen nineties, when banks were resisting ATM networks and early online banking, McKinsey Quarterly argued that banks could afford to wait because customer adoption was also slow. They won that round. The whole point of the twenty twenty six report is that the adoption curve has now decoupled from the institutional adoption curve. Generative AI hit forty five percent enterprise adoption in two years versus fifteen for digital banking. The grace period collapsed to roughly zero. The three speed bank is the structural response. The bigger frame is that for the first time the firm is openly saying that the next round of competitive separation in banking will not be driven by capital allocation. It will be driven by which institutions can sit comfortably with three different operating tempos under one roof. For your PE lens, the same logic transfers to portfolio companies. Operators who can hold a stable core and an aggressive capability layer in parallel will compound. Operators who treat AI as a project, not a tempo, will not.

Deep dive two. The MFN pricing analog and why it matters more than the headline.

The TrumpRx three hundred forty six dollar Zepbound number is the headline. The actual structural story is that this rhymes with the nineteen ninety Omnibus Reconciliation Act Section nineteen twenty seven, the Medicaid Best Price rule. That rule required pharma to offer Medicaid the lowest price given to any commercial buyer. The unintended consequence over the next decade was the modern pharmacy benefit manager rebate complex. Manufacturers stopped offering deep commercial discounts because they would be required to match for Medicaid. The behavior bent around the rule. Watch closely whether MFN at three hundred forty six becomes a new effective floor for commercial contracting and whether payers respond with MFN plus rebate demands. If that happens, you are watching the early innings of a second great PBM restructure, and the long tail of every pharmacy and managed care PE thesis on your desk needs to be reread. The honest read is that the political win is the easy part. The market structure response is what determines whether GLP one margins compress by twenty percent or by sixty percent over the next thirty six months. Lilly's M and A pipeline, Centessa and Insilico style bets, become necessary, not optional.

Deep dive three. Anthropic at nine hundred sixty five billion and the workflow contract economy.

The push back on the valuation is the obvious one. Forty seven billion of run rate revenue is real, but it is run rate, not annualized GAAP, and Anthropic is throttling third party agent usage right now because compute costs are biting. Acknowledge that. The deeper read is that this is the first round in which late stage AI capital is being deployed with the financing structures of late stage private equity. Sixty five billion dollar checks from four sponsors. Concentrated cap tables. Governance terms that look more like buyout deals than venture rounds. That is the financing innovation. The product innovation is that the locus of competition has moved from per seat chat licenses to billed spend share on agent workflows. The Ramp index showing thirty four percent for Anthropic versus thirty two percent for OpenAI, with Anthropic gaining four points in a month, is the market telling you which architectural bet is currently winning. If you are diligencing any back office automation business in the next eighteen months, the question to underwrite is which model provider's agent layer your target sits on top of, because that is now a counterparty risk.
