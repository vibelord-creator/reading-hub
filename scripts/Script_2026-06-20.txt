Deep dive one. Volcker, nineteen seventy-nine, meet Warsh, twenty twenty-six. When a new Fed chair spends his first meetings establishing anti-inflation credibility, that's the Volcker playbook, and it's worth understanding why it works. When Paul Volcker arrived in nineteen seventy-nine, he pivoted the Fed hard toward price stability and reset market expectations through sheer signaling, before the inflation data confirmed the turn. The lesson institutional memory took from that era is that a new chair sets credibility by what he removes, not by what the CPI prints next month. Warsh deleting the cut bias on day one rhymes with that. He's spending his credibility up front to anchor expectations. And here's the subtle strategic point, the one that elevates this from a news fact to a framework: the more credible the signal, the less the Fed may actually have to do. If markets believe you'll hike, financial conditions tighten on their own, inflation expectations stay anchored, and the central bank gets the tightening it wants without moving the policy rate as far. So the deletion of two words from a statement can do real monetary work. That's the Volcker insight. But let me give you the honest counterweight, because the analogy isn't free: Volcker had to take fed funds to twenty percent and tolerate two recessions to win that credibility. Signaling only works until it's tested. If energy keeps climbing and the market calls Warsh's bluff, he either has to actually hike into a slowing economy or lose the credibility he just spent. The Volcker rhyme cuts both ways, and the bear case is that the second verse is a hard landing.

Deep dive two. Bain's healthcare PE numbers through the lens of the Granularity of Growth. This is a two-thousand-eight McKinsey framework from Baghai, Smit, and Viguerie, and it's one of the most useful tools you can carry into a diligence conversation. The core idea: returns and growth don't live at the level of "the sector." They cluster in specific sub-segments, what the authors call cells. They tell you that if you underwrite "healthcare PE" at the sector level, you'll miss where the money actually is. You have to disaggregate to the cell, tech-enabled RCM, automation, specific specialty verticals, before you underwrite. The discipline is to never let a sector average seduce you.
