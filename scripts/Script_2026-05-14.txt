Deep dive one. The Warsh confirmation and the rate-hike repricing should be read together through the Volcker-Reagan parallel. In August 1979, Paul Volcker took the Fed chair with inflation re-accelerating and political pressure from the Carter administration to keep rates accommodative. Volcker chose to hike anyway. The fed funds rate went from an average of eleven point two percent in 1979 to twenty percent by June 1981. Prime rate hit twenty-one and a half percent. The United States suffered two recessions before inflation broke from fourteen point eight percent in March 1980 to under three percent by 1983. The cost was real. The benefit was that Volcker's choice re-anchored Federal Reserve credibility for two full decades, and that credibility arguably enabled the disinflation that drove the asset price returns of the 1980s and 1990s.

Now translate to today. The 2026 setup is genuinely gentler. CPI is at three point eight, not Volcker's roughly thirteen percent. But the credibility test is structurally identical. Warsh inherits the chair this Friday with the President's expectation of cuts and the data's demand for hikes.
