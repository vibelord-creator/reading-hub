That's the sweep. Two deep dives.

First deep dive. The Fed June seventeenth meeting is the cleanest historical-template test in macroeconomics right now. The current setup — three and a half to three and three quarters percent policy rate, an active energy shock, post-BLS-credibility scare, incoming chair — maps almost perfectly onto two distinct precedents that ended in very different places. In nineteen ninety-five, Greenspan held briefly, the oil and commodity scare faded, the Fed cut into what became a five-year expansion. That is the soft-landing template. In nineteen seventy-three, Burns kept policy too loose under political pressure as Yom Kippur drove oil up four-fold. Burns lost the room. The Fed's credibility did not recover for a decade. The June seventeenth SEP is the cleanest read we will get on which template the FOMC is running. The partner-level framing for your career is this. When you sit in a healthcare PE diligence in twenty twenty-six and someone asks about the hold period, you cannot answer it without a view on which template Warsh is running. If it is Greenspan ninety-five, five-year holds remain fine and underwriting at fourteen percent IRR is reasonable. If it is Burns seventy-three, you are underwriting seven-year holds at minimum and discounting at fifteen to sixteen. Same business, very different IRR. The cost of being wrong on the template is roughly two hundred basis points of expected return per year. That is enough to change which deals you do and which ones you pass on.

Second deep dive. The agentic AI in healthcare back-office narrative now has eight or more entries in your Key Themes database since January. The framework worth committing to memory is McKinsey's: reimagine, don't layer. If you bolt a gen-AI tool onto an existing workflow, you get marginal productivity. If you redesign the workflow around the agent — letting the agent own the loop, escalate by exception, report by outcome — you get fifteen to fifty percent EBITDA improvement in administrative spend. The empirical evidence is stacking up. Half of US health systems are implementing gen AI. Three workflows — revenue cycle, prior authorization, nursing documentation — concentrate the value. The PE play is in two layers. The first layer is the workflow-software incumbents who can be enriched with agentic capabilities and re-sold at a higher multiple. The second layer is the new entrants who built native agentic platforms and are racing to install before incumbents can catch up. The risk in layer one is that incumbents underestimate the workflow redesign required. The risk in layer two is that incumbents move faster than expected because the workflow redesign is, for a multibillion-dollar EHR vendor, a six-quarter problem and not a six-year problem. The right hedge is to take positions in both layers and let the workflow data settle the question.
