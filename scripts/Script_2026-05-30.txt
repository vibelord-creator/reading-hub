Now the deep dives. Two stories with historical context and frameworks.

First deep dive. The Burns to Volcker handoff of 1978–79 as a parallel for today's data credibility moment. When Arthur Burns left the Fed in March 1978 under accusations of having politically accommodated Nixon's inflation, the market, not the Fed, repriced the credibility of US inflation data. Real yields rose roughly one hundred fifty basis points in nine months before Volcker's October 1979 shift. The source is Allan Meltzer's "A History of the Federal Reserve, Volume Two, Book Two." The parallel today is exact in structure, even if the magnitudes differ. If BLS data integrity gets questioned, the credibility repricing happens in the bond market before the political fight resolves. The risk is not a single hot CPI print. It is the slow erosion of the term premium. And the cleanest test of whether that is happening is the spread between BLS prints and private surveys, specifically Atlanta Fed GDPNow, ADP payrolls, and the JOLTS revisions. Volcker proved that under stagflation, inflation expectations depend on whether markets believe the central bank will accept pain. Pre-Volcker, the Fed had threatened tightening multiple times and backed off. Markets stopped listening. Warsh now sits in the exact credibility-test seat Volcker occupied in October 1979. If he signals hikes and follows through, expectations reset cheaply. If he flinches, the path that breaks them gets steeper later. Cost of flinching compounds.

Second deep dive. Anthropic's vertical integration into compute. The forty-five billion dollar SpaceX deal is the largest non-hyperscaler AI infrastructure commitment ever disclosed. Read it through two frameworks at once. First, McKinsey's value migration framework. The model layer is commoditizing. Google priced Gemini Flash at roughly one-third of frontier. Anthropic's response is to take the value that is migrating away from the model and route it into the infrastructure underneath. They are not buying chips. They are buying option value on the next two to three years of inference demand at a locked unit cost. Second, think of this as Bain's PE infrastructure financialization. Compute is being financed like a power purchase agreement. Anchor tenant signs a multi-year off-take. The off-take re-rates the entire neocloud and data-center PE complex. CoreWeave, Lambda, Crusoe, all the comparable platforms now have a visible benchmark for what an anchor tenant pays. The honest critique is that this concentrates risk. If Anthropic's revenue trajectory disappoints, SpaceX is left holding compute capacity with no second buyer at that price point. The IPO timing of June 12 is not coincidental. SpaceX wants public-market liquidity before that risk gets re-examined.

Third deep dive. Granularity of Growth applied to healthcare. The 2008 McKinsey book by Baghai, Coley, and White made one core argument. Aggregate sector growth masks roughly sixty percent of the value created at the sub-segment level. Apply that lens to today's healthcare picture. Aggregate margins are compressing. UnitedHealth is under regulatory pressure. Hospital margins are still below pre-pandemic levels in most markets. The narrative read is "healthcare is hard right now." The granular read tells you a different story. Health Services and Technology, HST, is growing at eight percent revenue CAGR. Provider-led health plans have a twenty billion dollar untapped commercial pool. Primary care has the equivalent of forty-five million Americans with inadequate coverage, especially rural. GLP-1 demand is creating ancillary need for care coordination wrappers. Three of the four sub-pools are growing double-digits while the aggregate compresses. That is granularity of growth as applied analysis. The job for a 2026 healthcare PE thesis is to identify the two or three sub-pools that grow even if the aggregate misses, and underwrite into those. It is the same logic that made Oak Street and One Medical work in 2019 even when overall primary-care economics looked broken. The risk is over-fitting. Sub-pool theses can look attractive in 2026 and still get crushed by a single CMS rule change in 2028. Pair the granularity work with regulatory scenario analysis or the thesis will not survive contact with the next administration.
