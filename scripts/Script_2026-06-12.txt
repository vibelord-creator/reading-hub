Deep dive number four, and this one ties straight back to your coursework: the budget-versus-strategy argument, with some history attached. McKinsey's claim that the annual budget kills strategy isn't new wisdom, it's a restatement of a problem corporate finance has wrestled with for decades. The reason dynamic resource reallocation works is grounded in an old empirical finding: companies that meaningfully shift capital between business units over time dramatically outperform companies that hold their allocations roughly fixed year after year. The intuition is straightforward. Most management teams set this year's budget by taking last year's budget and adjusting at the margin, which means capital stays anchored to where the business was, not where it's going. The active reallocators behave like portfolio managers: they concentrate on the handful of variables that actually drive performance variance and they move money toward them aggressively. For you, the bridge is direct. This is exactly the muscle a private equity sponsor installs at a portfolio company on day one, and it's the same logic as the capital-budgeting and cost-of-capital work in corporate finance, where the discipline is to fund the projects whose returns clear the weighted-average cost of capital and starve the ones that don't, every single year, not just at acquisition. The partner-ready version: inertia in the budget process is a structural source of alpha for an active owner, because most boards are still running a process designed to defend last year.
