AVA: Treasury is buying back roughly four billion per operation. Total coupon supply this quarter is north of seven hundred billion. Buybacks are a liquidity tool — they smooth trading in old, off-the-run bonds. They do not remove supply, because Treasury funds the buyback by issuing new debt somewhere else on the curve. What's actually driving the thirty-year toward five and a quarter is the term premium — the extra yield investors demand to lock money up for thirty years instead of rolling short-term bills. ANDREW: Mechanism workshop. We do one concept properly each episode, and today it has to be duration, because it explains why this week's bond story is scarier than the headlines make it sound. Ava, the floor is yours.

AVA: All right. Duration is the answer to a simple question: if yields move one percentage point, how much does my bond's price move? For a thirty-year bond, the answer is roughly twenty percent. Not two. Twenty. Long bonds are levered to rate changes even with no leverage in the position, because you're locked into old coupons for three decades while the world reprices around you.

ANDREW: So walk through what that means for someone who bought thirty-year Treasuries at the lows.

AVA: Someone who bought the thirty-year in twenty twenty, when it yielded barely more than one percent, is sitting on a price loss of well over forty percent on the safest credit on earth. That's the quiet fact underneath every story we've done this week. It's why banks with big bond portfolios get nervous when the long end sells off — that was the Silicon Valley Bank lesson in twenty twenty-three. It's why pension funds, who are structurally forced to own duration, are the ones charging that rising term premium we keep talking about. And it's why a nine-basis-point rally on the buyback news was worth reporting at all: nine basis points on a thirty-year is real money — roughly one and a half percent of price.

ANDREW: And the connection to the AI debt story?

AVA: Direct. When hyperscalers issue thirty and forty-year infrastructure debt, they're selling duration into a market that's already choking on it from the Treasury. Every duration seller competes with every other duration seller, regardless of credit quality. So the marginal buyer of a data-center bond and the marginal buyer of a Treasury bond are, increasingly, the same investor with the same risk budget. That's the precise sense in which the AI boom moves mortgage rates — it's not a metaphor, it's a shared buyer.

ANDREW: One sentence for the networking version?

AVA: "Duration is the one risk you can't diversify inside the bond market — you can only price it, and right now the price is going up." If the person across the table nods slowly, they've traded bonds. If they change the subject, they haven't.
